Learn how to do it first

This is about multiplying decimals. You already know how to multiply whole numbers. Decimals need just ONE extra step at the end: put the decimal point in the right place. That is the part to learn.

Step 1. Hide the decimal points. Multiply the numbers like whole numbers.
Example: 54.6 × 0.7 becomes 546 × 7 = 3822.
Step 2. Count the digits after the decimal point in BOTH numbers, and add them up.
54.6 has 1 digit after the point, 0.7 has 1. Together that is 2.
Step 3. Put the decimal point in your answer so it has that many digits after it.
3822 with 2 digits after the point is 38.22. Done!
Your own sum: 54.6 × 0.7 = 38.22.
Maya wrote 382.2. That is 10 times too big! It only has 1 digit after the point, but it needs 2.
When you run out of digits: 1.65 × 0.03
Digits: 165 × 3 = 495. Decimal places: 2 + 2 = 4.
But 495 only has 3 digits! Add a zero at the front: 0.0495.
The bigger ones: 0.23 × 4.7
Split 4.7 into 4 and 0.7. 0.23 × 4 = 0.92. 0.23 × 0.7 = 0.161, NOT 1.61!
Remember, 0.7 means 7 tenths, so the answer is 10 times smaller than 0.23 × 7.
Add them: 0.92 + 0.161 = 1.081.
Quick check before you finish: estimate!
54.6 × 0.7 is about 55 × 0.7, which is about 38. So 38.22 makes sense, and 382.2 cannot be right.
If your answer is much bigger than your estimate, check the decimal point.
